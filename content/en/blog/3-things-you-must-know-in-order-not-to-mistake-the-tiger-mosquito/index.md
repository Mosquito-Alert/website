<h2>1. Like all mosquitos, its body is divided into three parts: head, thorax, and abdomen</h2>
[caption id="attachment_12028" align="alignright" width="237"]<img class="wp-image-12028" src="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/mosquito_icon_02.jpg" alt="Red: head with antennae; green: thorax with the legs and wings; blue: abdomen" width="237" height="243" /> Red: head with antennae; green: thorax with the legs and wings; blue: abdomen[/caption]
<p style="text-align: justify;">In order to identify a mosquito species it is crucial to understand the different parts of its body structure.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">The <strong>head</strong> is small, supporting the antennae and proboscis, which is the tubular mouthpart the females use to suck blood. The <strong>thorax</strong> is the part of the body from which emerge the three pairs of legs and wings. Finally, at the end of the body is the abdomen. This is the part that fills with blood after a female bites an animal or person.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">
</span></p>

<h2>2. White lines on the body are easily seen</h2>
<p style="text-align: justify;">The tiger mosquito is small and black, it has only one white line on the head and thorax, and it has several white lines on the abdomen and legs. Its behavior is unusual since it is active during the day, particularly in the afternoon, and almost never at night. It flies close to the ground and often bites on the legs.</p>


[caption id="attachment_12806" align="aligncenter" width="945"]<a href="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/layouts-blog.jpg"><img class="wp-image-12806 size-full" src="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/layouts-blog.jpg" alt="Comparative of the two mosquitos. <h2>3. There are other species similar to the tiger mosquito</h2>
&nbsp;
<h4><em>Culex pipiens</em></h4>
<p style="text-align: justify;">The common mosquito (<em>Culex pipiens</em>) is probably the mosquito with the greatest worldwide distribution. Its behavior is very different from that of the tiger mosquito since it bites at night, but it also bites indoors (this is probably the mosquito which keeps you up at night).</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">The adults are usually bigger than adult tiger mosquitos, and their body is light brown. They prefer to breed in dirty, stagnant water, including in different types of water drains, because they need water with a high organic matter content. The larvae have a very long aquatic siphon, which is a tube-shaped organ at the end of the larva’s abdomen used for breathing.</p>

<h4><em>Aedes geniculatus</em></h4>
[caption id="attachment_12030" align="alignright" width="263"]<a href="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/dvb-Aedes-geniculatus-1-21.jpg"><img class="wp-image-12030" src="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/dvb-Aedes-geniculatus-1-21.jpg" alt="Aedes geniculatus female, image courtesy of David Vilasís Boix." width="263" height="175" /></a> <em>Aedes geniculatus</em> female, image courtesy of David Vilasís Boix.[/caption]
<p style="text-align: justify;">This is the mosquito whose appearance is most similar to the tiger mosquito (Aedes albopictus). It generally lives in forested areas and is difficult to find in urban areas or houses. Ae. geniculatus has a few lines with golden hairs. Its legs aren’t covered with lines like the tiger mosquito, but it does have a single white spot just at the leg joint.</p>
&nbsp;

&nbsp;

<hr />

<h4><em>Aedes aegypti</em></h4>
[caption id="attachment_11920" align="alignright" width="267"]<a href="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/aedes-aegypti_mark-yokoyama_CC-BY-NC-ND-2.0.jpg"><img class="wp-image-11920 " src="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/aedes-aegypti_mark-yokoyama_CC-BY-NC-ND-2.0.jpg" alt="aedes aegypti_mark yokoyama_(CC BY-NC-ND 2.0)" width="267" height="177" /></a> Credit: Mark Yokoyama (CC BY-NC-ND 2.0)[/caption]
<p style="text-align: justify;">Another similar species is <em>Aedes aegypti</em>, the yellow fever mosquito. Like the tiger mosquito, this species is considered invasive in Europe, although its distribution is not as widespread, and it is not found in Spain. The thorax of <em>Ae. aegypti</em> (left) has silver scales in the shape of a lyre, while the mid section of the thorax of <em>Ae. albopictus</em> (right) has a single white line. Both species have a final segment of the back legs which is white.</p>


<hr />

<h4><em>Culiseta longiareolata</em></h4>
[caption id="attachment_5625" align="alignright" width="257"]<a href="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/C_longiareolata.jpg"><img class="wp-image-5625" src="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/C_longiareolata.jpg" alt="C_longiareolata" width="257" height="175" /></a> Foto: Roger Eritja[/caption]
<p style="text-align: justify;"><em>Culiseta longiareolata</em> is a very common mosquito species, sharing habitat with <em>Culex pipiens</em>, the common mosquito. Its thorax can be confused with that of the tiger mosquito, but looking carefully there is not only just one white line but a number of them. Also, cusileta is not black like the tiger mosquito; instead, its body is brown and large.</p>


<hr />

<h4 style="text-align: justify;"><em>Anopheles plumbeus</em></h4>
[caption id="attachment_12037" align="alignright" width="144"]<a href="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/Anopheles_plumbeus1.jpg"><img class=" wp-image-12037" src="http://www.mosquitoalert.com/wp-content/uploads/Anopheles_plumbeus1.jpg" alt="Anopheles plumbeus mosquito female biting on human skin" width="144" height="170" /></a> Anopheles plumbeus mosquito female biting on human skin[/caption]
<p style="text-align: justify;">This species is abundant in all of Europe, breeding in holes in the trunks of forest trees, but it can also breed in small containers around the household. It is well distinguished from the tiger mosquito by its color: it is not black, and it doesn’t have the characteristic white lines. It bites aggressively during the day.</p>
